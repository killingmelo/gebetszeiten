Gebetszeiten Islam shows which prayer is current, how much time is left and when the next one begins. That is all the app wants to do, and it should do it reliably. The app itself is in German.

OFFICIAL TIMES OR NONE AT ALL

The app fetches the official prayer times of Turkey's Diyanet and checks them against two independent sources. If the sources disagree, it says so. When nothing official exists for a day, you see a notice instead of a number. An estimated time is never slipped in quietly.

If you would rather see a time on such days, switch on the fallback in the settings. The app then computes those days itself (Diyanet method, Faǧr 18°, ʿIšāʾ 17°). Official times always take precedence.

Official tables for Germany are built in. They work without a network and cover more than 940 places.

NO ADS, NO ACCOUNT

No ads, no account, no tracking, no analytics, no location permission. You type in your place; the app never asks where you are. The only thing sent is the identifier of the place whose times are fetched; for technical reasons the server sees your IP address. The online sync can be switched off, and the built-in tables take over.

A version without any internet permission can also be built from the source code.

BATTERY

No permanent background service, no polling, no periodic refresh. The app wakes at prayer times and otherwise sleeps, usually about a dozen times a day. The widget changes only when something changes. Only if you switch on the Aḏān does a playback service run at prayer time, for as long as the Aḏān lasts.

The app asks to be exempted from battery optimisation. That sounds like the opposite, but the reason is simple: some manufacturers otherwise hold back reminders, and the prayer would be over before the reminder arrives. It does not make the app wake more often.

Optionally the app shows the time left until the next prayer as a status bar icon and as a line on the lock screen. That costs more wake-ups, and the setting says exactly how many.

FEATURES

* Today at a glance: current prayer with time left and progress, next prayer and every time of the day without scrolling; swipe to change the day, each row explains itself with a tap
* Understanding the times: a tab per prayer with start, end and Rakʿa, quotations from Hanafi scholars in Arabic and German, terms explained and a sundial for your own place
* Month view and Qibla compass that tells you whether it is accurate
* Makrūh times and voluntary prayers (Ḍuḥā, Auwābīn, Tahaǧǧud), if you want them
* Congregational prayer (Ǧamāʿa) in the morning, lead time adjustable
* Shift any time by up to 60 minutes if your mosque prays at a different time; always marked, also on the watch
* Favourite places for switching quickly
* Hijri date, adjustable text size, high contrast, night view in pure black
* Silent reminders per prayer; sound and vibration only if you switch them on
* Aḏān at prayer time, if you want it: per prayer, its own Aḏān for Faǧr, your own recording possible
* Widgets for home and lock screen: six colour themes, four layouts, opacity, time left and contents for each widget separately

WEAR OS

The watch shows the next prayer as a tile and as a complication. It takes the times from the paired phone and fetches them itself when needed. For that it has its own internet permission and no separate switch. Without a network it falls back on the same built-in tables.

NOTE

The times come from the Diyanet. The app itself is not published by the Diyanet and has no connection to it.
